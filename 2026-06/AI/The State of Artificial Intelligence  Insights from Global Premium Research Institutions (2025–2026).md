# The State of Artificial Intelligence: Insights from Global Premium Research Institutions (2025–2026)

***

## Executive Summary

Artificial intelligence has crossed from experimental technology into foundational business infrastructure. Across every major research institution — from management consultancies and investment banks to venture capital firms and technology analysts — a consensus is forming: the question is no longer *whether* to adopt AI, but *how fast* and *how deeply* to integrate it. AI adoption now touches 88% of organizations globally, global AI spending is projected to exceed $2 trillion in 2026, and a landmark new divide is widening between organizations that are redesigning operations around AI and those that are merely layering it on top of existing workflows.[^1][^2][^3]

This report synthesizes the most significant findings from more than 20 premium research institutions — including McKinsey, BCG, Deloitte, PwC, KPMG, EY, IBM, Accenture, JP Morgan, Citi, BlackRock, Forrester, Gartner, IDC, CB Insights, a16z, Palantir, Scale AI, Cohere, and Stanford HAI — covering AI adoption, economics, governance, workforce impact, technology trends, and investment activity.

***

## 1. AI Adoption: From Pilots to Scale

### The Adoption Surge

Artificial intelligence has reached near-universal organizational penetration. McKinsey's 2025 State of AI report found that **88% of organizations now use AI in at least one business function**, up dramatically from just 20% in 2017. Generative AI adoption alone nearly tripled from 33% in 2023 to 79% by late 2025. The Stanford HAI 2026 AI Index Report confirmed that industry produced over 90% of notable frontier models in 2025, and 4 in 5 university students now use generative AI.[^4][^1]

Despite broad adoption, depth of integration remains uneven. Only 38% of organizations have scaled AI beyond pilot programs, and KPMG found that while 48% of organizations report significant productivity gains from AI, only 11% report substantial enterprise-wide impact. Deloitte's 2026 State of AI in the Enterprise report confirmed that the number of companies with 40% or more of AI projects in production is set to double within six months, signaling a rapid shift toward deployment.[^5][^6][^1]

### The "Future-Built" Divide

The defining strategic tension of 2025–2026 is the widening gap between AI leaders and laggards. BCG's 2025 research found that only **5% of value-generating companies** — termed "future-built" — are achieving transformative impact from AI, delivering five times the revenue increases and three times the cost reductions compared to peers. AI leaders have demonstrated 1.7x revenue growth, 3.6x higher total shareholder return, and a 1.6x EBIT margin over three years. Conversely, 60% of companies report minimal revenue and cost gains despite substantial investment.[^7][^2]

Scale AI's landmark "Six Percent Report" (June 2026) found that just **6% of companies have made enterprise AI work at scale**. The three distinguishing traits of those that succeed are: (1) strong data foundations treating data quality and governance as core infrastructure; (2) front-loading organizational change management, employee training, and senior leadership sponsorship; and (3) a differentiated build-versus-buy approach rather than relying solely on off-the-shelf tools.[^8]

## 2. Economic Impact and Investment

### Macro-Level Projections

The macroeconomic potential of AI is staggering across all major financial and consulting institutions. PwC's *Value in Motion* report projects AI could **boost global GDP by up to 15 percentage points** over the next decade — on par with the growth impact of 19th-century industrialization — contingent on responsible deployment, governance, and public trust. In a pessimistic scenario with low trust and cooperation, the incremental boost falls to just 1%, underscoring how governance is not a compliance exercise but an economic multiplier.[^9]

JP Morgan estimates between **$5–7 trillion in global data center and AI infrastructure spend** over the next five years, with hyperscaler capex rising from $150 billion in 2023 to over $500 billion projected for 2026. BlackRock's 2026 *Pushing the Limits* report forecasts **$5–8 trillion in AI-related capital expenditure from 2025 to 2030**, with 2026 investments expected to contribute three times the historical average to U.S. economic growth. Citi Research estimates AI-related capex will reach **$7.75 trillion globally by 2030**.[^10][^11][^12][^13]

McKinsey sizes the long-term AI opportunity at **$4.4 trillion in added productivity growth potential** from corporate use cases alone. Agentic AI specifically could **boost productivity by 3–5% annually** and lift growth by 10% or more.[^14][^15]

### AI Spending by the Numbers

Gartner projects worldwide spending on AI will reach approximately **$2.59 trillion in 2026**, a 47% increase year over year, with AI infrastructure accounting for more than 45% of total global spending. The top six AI markets in 2025 include GenAI-enabled smartphones ($298B), AI services ($283B), AI-optimized servers ($268B), AI processing semiconductors ($209B), AI application software ($172B), and AI infrastructure software ($126B).[^3][^16]

IDC reports that AI infrastructure spending reached **$318 billion in full-year 2025**, more than doubling the $153 billion recorded in 2024. IDC projects spending will reach $487 billion in 2026 and surpass **$1 trillion by 2029** at a five-year CAGR of approximately 31%. Organizationally, KPMG found that enterprise AI leaders plan to invest an average of **$202 million over the next 12 months**.[^17][^18]

## 3. Venture Capital and Startup Ecosystem

### Record Investment Flows

CB Insights' State of Venture 2025 report documented that **AI companies raised $226 billion in 2025, representing 48% of all global venture funding** — the largest share on record. Total venture funding reached $469 billion in 2025, the highest level since 2022. The six largest funding rounds of 2025 all went to AI companies: OpenAI ($41B), Anthropic ($32.5B), Scale AI ($14.8B), xAI ($12.8B), Databricks ($5B), and Aligned ($5B) — together accounting for 49% of all AI funding and 24% of all venture capital.[^19]

Globally, private AI companies raised a record **$225.8 billion in 2025**, nearly double 2024's total, with Q4 2025 alone reaching $83.2 billion in quarterly funding. JP Morgan's H2 2025 Innovation Economy Update found that AI attracted an all-time high of 71% of investor interest in Q1 2025, up from just 21% in Q1 2020. The top 10 private company valuations collectively exceeded $2 trillion, with OpenAI ($500B) and Anthropic ($350B) commanding substantial premiums.[^20][^21][^22]

### The AI Stack Layers

a16z's Big Ideas 2026 series highlights the emergence of **agent-native infrastructure as table stakes**, with AI moving from a chat-first to an action-first paradigm. a16z identifies three major 2026 shifts: AI interfaces that observe and act proactively rather than waiting for prompts; design moving from human-first to agent-readable; and the rise of agentic execution across enterprise workflows. The firm is actively tracking 40+ startup ideas including multimodal data management, AI-revived cybersecurity, and AI as a civilization-level technology.[^23][^24][^25][^26]

Cohere, focused exclusively on enterprise AI, achieved **$240 million in annual recurring revenue in 2025**, exceeding its $200 million target, with consistent quarter-over-quarter growth exceeding 50%. Palantir reported Q1 2026 revenue of $1.633 billion, representing **85% year-over-year growth** — the company's highest growth rate ever as a public company — as its AI Platform (AIP) and Foundry became the operating layer for enterprise AI decisions. Databricks was recognized by IDC's 2025 MarketScape as a Leader in unified AI governance platforms, cited for comprehensive agentic AI governance capabilities.[^27][^28][^29][^30]

## 4. Agentic AI: The Next Frontier

### From GenAI to Agents

Virtually every major research institution identifies **agentic AI** — AI systems that can plan, reason, and execute multi-step tasks autonomously — as the defining shift of 2026. Deloitte's 2026 report notes that agentic AI usage is poised to rise sharply in the next two years, but only one in five companies has a mature governance model for autonomous AI agents. Gartner's Top 10 Technology Trends for 2026 highlights multiagent systems as the era where single copilots give way to "coordinated agent swarms" requiring orchestration, governance, and composability.[^31][^5]

Forrester's 2026 predictions frame enterprise applications as moving beyond tools for humans to accommodating a **"digital workforce" of AI agents**, with the top five HCM platforms expected to offer digital employee management capabilities. Thirty percent of enterprise application vendors are expected to launch their own MCP (Model Context Protocol) servers, and half of enterprise ERP vendors will launch autonomous governance modules combining explainable AI with real-time compliance monitoring.[^32]

Accenture's analysis of banking trends for 2026 found that over the next three years, **57% of banking executives expect AI agents to be fully embedded in risk, compliance, and audit functions**, as well as fraud detection and transaction monitoring. Gartner estimates that 40% of enterprise applications will include AI agents by 2029, up from less than 1% in 2024.[^33][^34]

### Citi's Compound AI Vision

Citi Ventures' 2026 perspective on generative AI articulates the emergence of **"compound AI" architecture** — systems that blend probabilistic reasoning from LLMs with deterministic rule-based systems — to deliver more trustworthy enterprise automation. Citi predicts enterprises will increasingly become "mini LLM companies," actively curating proprietary data to fine-tune models for their specific workflows and industries. Agentic AI systems that can make independent decisions — from managing supply chains to executing investment strategies — are forecast to move from vision to practical reality.[^35]

## 5. AI ROI and the Value Gap

### The ROI Paradox

Despite massive investment, demonstrating clear financial returns from AI remains a central challenge across institutions. PwC's 29th Global CEO Survey found that more than **half (56%) of CEOs say their company has seen neither higher revenues nor lower costs from AI** over the last 12 months. Only 30% report increased revenue and 26% report lower costs. BCG's 2026 AI Radar — surveying 2,300+ executives across 21 markets — found that AI investment is projected to double from 0.8% to 1.7% of revenues in 2026, and 94% of CEOs plan to continue investing regardless of short-term returns.[^36][^37]

Forrester's 2026 Predictions label this the **"Era of Evaluation"** — enterprises will defer 25% of planned AI spending as CFOs demand demonstrable returns, moving AI from a tiara to a hard hat. KPMG's findings show that 65% of UK respondents say their organization would continue investing in AI regardless of tangible ROI, revealing that traditional return on investment calculations have become inadequate measures for AI value.[^38][^39][^40]

### JPMorgan: The ROI Benchmark

JPMorgan Chase stands as the clearest case study of measurable AI ROI at enterprise scale. CEO Jamie Dimon stated that the firm's **$2 billion annual AI investment has "paid for itself"** — tracking 600+ individual use cases with defined metrics. LLM Suite users report saving an average of four hours per day; AI-driven trading systems improved win rates from 52% to 63% and saved $25 million in slippage costs; and AI created $1–1.5 billion in value through customer personalization, fraud detection, and credit decisioning. JPMorgan has reclassified AI from innovation investment to **core infrastructure**, on par with payment processing and risk management.[^41][^42]

## 6. Workforce and Organizational Impact

### The Labor Market Transformation

PwC's 2026 Global AI Jobs Barometer — analyzing over one billion job ads from six continents — reveals AI is creating a **two-track labor market**. AI is "professionalizing" some jobs by requiring even more human expertise, while "democratizing" others by making them accessible to non-experts. Critically, new tasks added to AI-exposed roles are **2.5 times more likely to rely on skills like empathy, judgment, and creativity**. The wage premium for AI-skilled workers rose to **34.2% in 2025**, up from 11% the prior year.[^43][^44]

IBM's 2026 CEO Study (surveying 2,000 CEOs across 33 geographies) found that **76% of organizations now have a Chief AI Officer (CAIO)**, up from just 26% in 2025 — a near tripling in 12 months. CEOs expect **48% of operational decisions to be made by AI without human input by 2030**, doubling from roughly 25% today. IBM found that AI-first CEOs have delivered **17% higher revenue growth** over the past three years compared to peers.[^45][^46][^47]

### Skills and Culture

Deloitte's 2026 report identifies **insufficient worker skills as the biggest barrier** to AI integration, with 53% of organizations educating the broader workforce to raise AI fluency. Deloitte's cultural debt analysis warns that organizations building AI-first strategies without assessing cultural and trust impacts risk "cultural debt" — eroding adoption over time, where trust (not ambition or access) will ultimately determine whether organizations can scale AI or stall.[^48][^5]

KPMG's global trust and AI study found that while AI is already embedded in daily work, it is often without governance, transparency, or leadership intent, with only approximately **13% of organizations** having scaled GenAI use across the enterprise. To lift adoption, Forrester predicts that **30% of large enterprises will mandate AI training** in 2026 to reduce risk and improve AI literacy.[^49][^50]

## 7. AI Governance, Trust, and Risk

### The Governance Gap

Governance is the single most cited gap between AI ambition and AI impact. McKinsey's 2026 State of AI Trust report found that the average Responsible AI (RAI) maturity score rose to 2.3 in 2026, up from 2.0 in 2025, but only about **one-third of organizations** report maturity in responsible AI practices. The McKinsey AI Trust Maturity Model now includes a new dimension specifically for **agentic AI governance and controls**, reflecting the growing importance of governing increasingly autonomous systems.[^51]

Gartner's 2026 strategic trends identify AI Security Platforms as a top priority, framing models, pipelines, and APIs as "first-class risk objects" with AI risk moving to the boardroom. Gartner also introduces "digital provenance" — the ability to trace where data, models, and decisions came from — as a non-optional trust requirement, especially in regulated industries. IBM's 2026 Enterprise Outlook forecasts that between 2025 and 2030, business leaders predict **AI investment will surge approximately 150% as a percentage of revenue**, amplifying governance stakes proportionally.[^52][^53][^31]

### The EU AI Act and Regulatory Pressure

EY's 2026 CEO Outlook (surveying CEOs globally) found that **around 80% of CEOs report increased AI investment in 2026**, with the focus shifting explicitly from adoption to demonstrable value and ROI. EY emphasizes that AI's competitive advantage will depend on how well it is measured against business performance, not just technology deployment metrics. With the EU AI Act enforcement commencing, Forrester predicts 60% of Fortune 100 companies will appoint dedicated AI Governance heads and legal claims involving AI will exceed 2,000 cases.[^54][^55]

## 8. Technology Landscape and Key Trends

### McKinsey's 13 Technology Trends for 2025

McKinsey's Technology Trends Outlook 2025 identifies **13 breakthrough trends** grouped under three themes:[^56]

**AI Revolution**
- **Agentic AI**: Autonomous multi-step decision-makers acting as virtual coworkers; job postings grew 985% from 2023 to 2024 with $1.1 billion in equity investment[^57]
- **Artificial Intelligence**: The primary driver of digital transformation and the "great amplifier" accelerating all other technology trends[^58]

**Compute and Connectivity Frontiers**
- Application-specific semiconductors (AI chips), advanced connectivity (5G, LEO satellites, edge), cloud and edge computing (global data center demand growing 19–22% annually to 2030), immersive reality, digital trust and cybersecurity, quantum technologies[^56]

**Cutting-Edge Engineering**
- Future of robotics, mobility, bioengineering, space technologies, and energy/sustainability technologies

### Gartner's Top 10 Strategic Tech Trends for 2026

Gartner's 2026 Technology Trend Playbook emphasizes that **AI is no longer a feature but the substrate upon which the enterprise is built**:[^31][^52]

1. **AI-Native Development Platforms** — AI becomes the development substrate; less code, higher abstraction
2. **AI Supercomputing Platforms** — Compute, energy, cost, and sovereignty become strategic decisions
3. **Confidential Computing** — Zero trust moves to default; data protected even during processing
4. **Multiagent Systems** — Single copilots give way to orchestrated agent swarms
5. **Domain-Specific Language Models** — Competitive advantage shifts to proprietary, industry-grade data
6. **Physical AI** — Intelligence moves into robotics, drones, and smart machinery
7. **Preemptive Cybersecurity** — Security shifts from detect-and-respond to anticipate-and-neutralize
8. **Digital Provenance** — Trust becomes traceable across data, models, and decisions
9. **AI Security Platforms** — AI risk moves to boardrooms as first-class governance objects
10. **Geopatriation** — Workloads shift by regulation, sovereignty, and resilience, not just cost[^52]

### Accenture's Technology Vision 2025

Accenture's Technology Vision 2025, based on insights from 3,000+ C-level executives, identifies a **"Binary Big Bang"** driven by AI at the core. Four defining trends are: AI-driven reinvention, brand-personified AI (95% of leaders see a consistent AI personality as essential to brand trust), robotics-powered transformation (76% of executives see the promise of adaptable intelligent robots), and workforce collaboration between humans and AI. Nearly 70% of executives say embodied AI — systems that sense, move, and act — will force them to reinvent how work is designed.[^59][^60][^61]

## 9. Sector-Specific AI Applications

### Financial Services

JP Morgan has embedded AI across 150,000 employees weekly for coding, marketing, and risk assessment, with an in-house large language model developed since 2012. Jamie Dimon's 2025 shareholder letter states AI "will affect virtually every function, application, and process in the company" and its pace of adoption "will likely be far faster than prior transformations, like electricity or the internet". Accenture's Top Banking Trends for 2026 found that banks are building AI agents aligned with compliance and service standards, with 57% of banking executives expecting agentic AI embedded in risk functions within three years.[^42][^34]

Citi has deployed two internal chatbot assistants, provided 30,000 developers with coding tools via Google Cloud's Vertex AI, and launched Agent Assist for customer service in personal banking. AI reduced Citi's document review time from one hour to 15 minutes for account onboarding. BlackRock maintains a sustained bias toward major AI names, framing AI as "dominant" in markets and central to portfolio strategy through the iShares AI Innovation and Tech Active ETF.[^11][^62][^63]

### Enterprise Operations

IBM's 2026 Enterprise in 2030 report projects AI investment will surge 150% (as a percentage of revenue) between 2025 and 2030. IBM's AI adoption challenges report identifies data quality, governance, skills gaps, and workflow integration as the top barriers to scaling from GenAI pilots to enterprise-wide agentic AI. IBM's generative AI-related bookings surpassed $6 billion in Q1 2025.[^64][^53][^65]

Palantir's Artificial Intelligence Platform (AIP) has become the operating layer for enterprise AI decisions, with its Ontology creating digital replicas of organizations by mapping real-world relationships between objects, decisions, and actions. Palantir's Q1 2026 commercial revenue growth reached 137% year over year, with the company serving as the AI operating system for defense and enterprise customers.[^66][^67][^68]

## 10. AI in the Physical World: Robotics and Physical AI

A convergence theme identified across McKinsey, Gartner, Accenture, and a16z is the expansion of AI from digital applications into the physical world. CB Insights documented that **robotics companies achieved a record $40.7 billion in funding in 2025**, representing 9% of total venture funding, with industrial humanoid robots leading with 80 deals. Gartner calls this "Physical AI" — intelligence extending into robotics, drones, smart machinery, and embedded intelligence where digital technologies intersect with mechanical systems.[^22][^52]

McKinsey highlights robotics as one of its 13 technology trends, while Accenture notes that AI-driven robots now offer efficiency, safety, and human collaboration, with roles in warehouse coordination and frontline logistics being reshaped by agentic systems acting in the physical world. a16z's Big Ideas 2026 identifies autonomous labs as the frontier, where AI reasoning and robotics together accelerate scientific discovery — with AI models already autonomously solving PhD-level science questions and Putnam competition mathematics.[^69][^59]

## 11. Responsible AI and the Trust Imperative

### Why Trust Is the Differentiator

Across all institutions, **trust** emerges as the meta-theme of 2026 AI strategy. McKinsey's RAI framework emphasizes that agentic AI introduces a new class of risk — systems not just *saying* the wrong thing but *doing* the wrong thing through unintended actions, tool misuse, or operating beyond appropriate guardrails. Forrester's 2026 predictions describe AI as trading its "tiara for a hard hat" — moving from innovation hype to operational discipline, governance, and training mandates.[^39][^50][^51]

EY's 2026 report on AI in consumer products notes that while AI capabilities are expanding rapidly, commercial decision-making remains deliberately human-led, with advanced organizations designing systems where AI augments human judgment — improving speed and consistency while maintaining accountability. KPMG's report emphasizes the need for an enterprise AI strategy, AI governance, and enterprise trust as the three pillars of sustainable transformation.[^6][^70]

### Governance Frameworks Maturing

IBM's 2026 CEO study notes that 83% of CEOs agree AI sovereignty — control over their own AI models, data, and infrastructure — is essential to business strategy. Deloitte's research shows only one in five companies has a mature governance model for autonomous AI agents despite agentic AI set to grow sharply. Scale AI's Six Percent Report and Databricks' recognition as an IDC Leader in unified AI governance both underscore that governance infrastructure is now a competitive capability, not a compliance checkbox.[^47][^5][^27][^8]

BCG's 2026 AI Radar found that **65% of CEOs now rank accelerating AI among their top three priorities**, while 82% say they are more optimistic about AI's ROI than they were a year ago. AI investment is projected to double as a percentage of revenues in 2026, and approximately **90% of CEOs believe AI agents will deliver measurable ROI in 2026**. This marks a definitive transition from a period of experimentation and hype to one of scaled deployment and accountability.[^71][^37][^72]

EY's 2026 CEO Outlook confirms that while CEOs are not idealizing AI, they are becoming more operationally serious about it — shifting from whether to adopt AI to how and where to scale it for the biggest financial return. AI now appears in CEO agendas alongside geopolitics and supply chain resilience as a structural, long-term driver of enterprise value creation.[^54]

## 13. Synthesis: The Five Strategic Imperatives

Drawing across all 20+ institutions, five imperatives emerge for organizations navigating AI in 2026 and beyond:

**1. Redesign, Don't Overlay.** The organizations extracting the most value from AI are not layering it onto existing workflows but redesigning operations around AI capabilities. BCG's future-built framework, IBM's AI-first transformation study, and McKinsey's agentic AI productivity research all converge on this point.[^2][^73][^15]

**2. Build Governance as Infrastructure.** Governance is no longer a legal checkbox — it is the mechanism by which AI trust is built, risk is managed, and regulatory exposure is controlled. McKinsey's RAI framework, Gartner's AI Security Platforms trend, and Scale AI's Six Percent Report all identify governance as a core competitive differentiator.[^51][^8][^31]

**3. Invest in Agents Strategically.** Agentic AI will be the dominant enterprise AI mode by 2029. Gartner, Deloitte, Forrester, Citi, and Accenture all forecast agentic adoption as the next wave, but each cautions that agent deployment without clear human oversight thresholds, accountability structures, and evaluation frameworks will compound failures at scale.[^34][^5][^32][^35]

**4. Treat Data as Foundation, Not Fuel.** Scale AI's research and KPMG's findings both identify data quality and readiness as the primary bottleneck separating AI leaders from laggards. Organizations that treat data governance, labeling, lineage, and quality as infrastructure — not as a one-time project — outperform on AI outcomes.[^64][^8]

**5. Measure at the Use Case Level.** JPMorgan's approach of tracking 600+ individual AI use cases with defined metrics, rather than seeking a single "AI ROI" figure, is emerging as the gold standard. Forrester, KPMG, and EY all caution that traditional ROI frameworks are inadequate for AI — new measurement architectures that capture time recovery, quality improvements, risk reduction, and revenue attribution are required.[^40][^38][^41][^54]
