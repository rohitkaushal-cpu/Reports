### The Shift: From Scripted Bots to LLM-Native Enterprise Agents

Enterprise chatbot development has undergone a fundamental architectural transformation. The traditional scripted-flow chatbot is being replaced by LLM-native enterprise agents that:

- Understand natural language queries without rigid intent trees
- Retrieve context from enterprise knowledge bases using RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)[^21][^22]

RAG is the dominant enterprise architecture because it allows the LLM to consult current databases and documents, returning up-to-date, specific information without requiring model retraining. It offers scalability, security, and reliability in delivering accurate answers — and enterprises favour it because it is easier to govern.[^23][^21]

### The Technology Stack

Modern IDP goes far beyond basic OCR. The full IDP lifecycle operates as an eight-step pipeline:[^40]

1. **Ingestion** — Multi-format document capture (PDF, image, email, scan)
2. **Preprocessing** — Image enhancement, de-skewing, noise reduction
3. **Classification** — AI-based document type identification
4. **Data Extraction** — OCR + deep learning for structured and unstructured fields
5. **Post-processing** — Normalisation, entity recognition, data standardisation
6. **Validation** — Rules-based and ML-based accuracy checks
7. **Human-in-the-Loop Review** — Configurable exception routing for human validation
8. **System Integration + Feedback Loop** — ERP/CRM posting and continuous model improvement

IDP reduces human error by over 90%, processes documents in seconds versus hours, and ensures full audit trails for compliance. The key distinction from traditional RPA: IDP understands variable document layouts where RPA fails; most enterprises pair the two for end-to-end automation.[^41][^40]

### Why Enterprises Fail at IDP in Production

The same failure patterns repeat consistently:[^42][^40]

- Models cannot generalise across diverse document types at scale
- Disconnected systems create gaps between capture, extraction, and ERP posting
- No governed review layer for human validation and exceptions
- No continuous improvement feedback mechanism
- Data quality issues in source systems undermine extraction accuracy

This is exactly why Antier's "volume-first architecture, workflow-led design, governance-first orientation" positioning is validated by market evidence — it directly addresses the four most common failure modes.

The enterprise IDP market is dominated by platform players: Tungsten (TotalAgility), ABBYY Vantage, UiPath Document Understanding, Microsoft Azure AI Document Intelligence, Google Cloud Document AI, Amazon Textract, Hyperscience, and Rossum. ## 8. AI Governance & Security

### Market Context

The AI governance market is one of the fastest-emerging segments in enterprise technology. The global market was valued at $309.01 million in 2025 and is predicted to grow from $419.45 million in 2026 to approximately $5.88 billion by 2035 at a CAGR of 34.27%. Large enterprises dominate at 70% of market share in 2025. Growth drivers include: rising integration of AI in regulated industries, growing emphasis on ethical AI certification programmes, data localisation mandates globally, and AI vendor risk management protocols.[^57]

### The Regulatory Imperative: EU AI Act

The EU AI Act — the world's first comprehensive legal framework for AI — entered into force on 1 August 2024 and its majority provisions became applicable in August 2026. Full compliance is required by 2 August 2027. This creates an immediate, compliance-driven demand for AI governance services across every enterprise deploying AI.[^58][^59][^60]

Key EU AI Act requirements enterprises must meet:[^60]

- **Risk classification**: AI systems classified into unacceptable, high, limited, and minimal risk tiers
- **AI Risk Management Systems (RMS)**: Covering design, testing, and post-market monitoring
- **Human oversight and data governance controls**: Mandatory for high-risk AI
- **Transparency requirements**: For chatbots, deepfakes, and other AI-generated content
- **Registration of high-risk AI systems** in the EU database
- **Continuous post-market monitoring and audit records**

Penalties for non-compliance reach up to €35 million or 7% of global annual turnover — whichever is higher. This makes AI governance commercially urgent, not optional.[^59][^58]

### AI Governance in 2026: From Policy to Operating Model

The critical 2026 insight is that AI governance is evolving from a policy document into an operating model. As AI-driven and agentic decision-making becomes embedded in day-to-day operations, governance can no longer live in steering committees alone — it must be embedded into every deployment, workflow, and model lifecycle management process.[^13]

### The Four Governance Failure Modes

Enterprises most commonly fail at AI governance in four ways:[^18][^13]

1. **No centralised AI policy or governance framework**: Scattered AI initiatives with no ownership
2. **Limited visibility into AI decisions and data usage**: Black-box models that cannot be explained or audited
3. **Growing exposure to compliance and audit failures**: No model cards, no risk scoring, no audit trails
4. **Insecure models vulnerable to misuse**: Prompt injection, data leakage, unauthorised access in LLM deployments

### Antier's Governance Architecture

Antier's AI Governance service covers two interlocking pillars:

**Security Services**:
- AI model hardening and threat prevention
- Real-time misuse monitoring and threat detection
- Secure inference and data access controls
- Scalable security for multi-region operations

**Governance Services**:
- Centralised governance platforms with model lifecycle tracking
- Approval and review workflows with human checkpoints
- AI policy consulting aligned to EU AI Act, GDPR, sector-specific regulation
- Risk scoring, audit readiness dashboards, and KPI reporting

**Responsible AI Controls**:
- Bias detection and mitigation
- Explainability and decision transparency frameworks
- Human-in-the-loop governance models
- Immutable audit logs, data lineage, and privacy enforcement

### The 12% That Succeed

The 12% of organisations that succeed with production AI share four attributes: pre-deployment infrastructure investment, governance documentation before deployment, baseline metrics captured before pilots, and dedicated business ownership with accountability for post-deployment performance. Antier's governance practice is the enabler of that 12% success rate — making governance not a cost centre but the prerequisite for realising AI ROI.[^5]
