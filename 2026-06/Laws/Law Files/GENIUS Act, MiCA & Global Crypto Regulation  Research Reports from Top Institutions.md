# GENIUS Act, MiCA & Global Crypto Regulation: Research Reports from Top Institutions

## Overview

This report is a curated master list of research reports, white papers, PDFs, and articles on the **GENIUS Act** (U.S. stablecoin legislation), **MiCA** (EU Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation), and related global digital asset regulatory frameworks — sourced exclusively from tier-1 institutions: multilateral organizations, global banks, big-four consultancies, strategy firms, rating agencies, and central banking bodies.

The GENIUS Act (Guiding and Establishing National Innovation for U.S. Stablecoins) was signed into law on **July 18, 2025**, making it the first comprehensive federal framework for payment stablecoins in the United States. Simultaneously, the EU's **MiCA regulation** became fully effective on December 30, 2024, creating a harmonized legal framework for crypto-assets across 27 member states. These two frameworks represent the most consequential regulatory developments in global digital assets since blockchain's inception.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

## Thematic Research: Cross-Institutional Findings

### On Stablecoin Market Scale

- Stablecoin market capitalization reached approximately **$260–$300 billion** by mid-2025[^39][^20][^6]
- Stablecoin trading volume reached **$23 trillion** in 2024, up 90% year-over-year[^6]
- Transaction volumes surpassed **Visa and Mastercard combined** in 2024[^1]
- 99% of all stablecoins are pegged to the U.S. dollar[^47]

### On Tokenization Forecasts

- **Citi GPS** forecasts $5.5 trillion in tokenized assets by 2030 (base case)[^27]
- Current global tokenized asset market stands at approximately **$17 billion**[^27]
- **BCG/Anchorage** report identifies crypto brokerage generating **$30–60 billion** annually[^39]
- **Oliver Wyman** estimates institutional crypto revenues at most **$16.4 billion** by 2030[^42]
- McKinsey's Technology Trends Outlook 2025 confirms tokenized assets are moving "from experimentation to real-world financial markets"[^36]

### On Regulatory Divergence Risk

- The FSB's 2025 thematic review found that despite progress, "significant gaps and inconsistencies remain"[^7]
- The ESRB specifically flagged MiCA's inability to address cross-border multi-issuer stablecoin schemes[^12]
- The BIS found nearly 70% of central banks responding had active stablecoin or CBDC projects[^11]
- WEF calls for global standards, consumer protection frameworks, and public-private collaboration as priority next steps[^1]
